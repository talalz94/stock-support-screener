Small edge, not statistically established. Backtest: 1,300 flags, 2024-02 → 2026-08, entry at the next open, 2-ATR trailing stop → +2.16% mean per trade. But a random pick from the same eligible pool returned +1.60% under the identical rule, so the screen's edge is +0.56% — smaller than the ±0.96% standard error of the measurement (Welch p = 0.28: not distinguishable from random selection). Win rates are identical, 41% vs 41%. details
Returns are strongly right-skewed — median −3.7% against a +2.16% mean — so most trades lose a little and a few win big. Position sizing and taking every signal therefore matter more than picking. 11 of 12 exit rules favour the screen (sign-test p=0.003), which is weak directional evidence of something real, though the rules are highly correlated so that overstates it. Of 17 subgroup comparisons, zero survive a Bonferroni correction; the strongest is the RECENT age band (+3.09% edge, raw p=0.005, corrected p=0.088) — a lead worth watching, not a validated filter. Two design assumptions are contradicted: EARLY is the only bucket with a negative mean (−0.72%) despite being built to be the earliest entry, and support grade A (n=1011, +0.04% edge) is the weakest grade even though support quality carries the largest score weight. Costs, spread and borrow are not modelled and would plausibly consume a 0.5–0.8% edge on sub-$5 names. Survivorship makes every figure optimistic: the universe comes from Alpaca's current asset list, so companies that delisted mid-window were never fetched. Reproduce: python backtest.py --start 2024-02 --every 3 --exits then python baseline.py --seeds 10.
